Grading protects a card, verifies it's genuine, and — for the right cards — multiplies
its value. But the three big graders aren't interchangeable. Here's how PSA, CGC and BGS
compare for Pokémon.

## The three graders at a glance

- **PSA** — the market leader for Pokémon. A PSA 10 ("Gem Mint") is the most liquid,
  most in-demand slab, and usually commands the highest resale premium for Pokémon
  cards specifically.
- **CGC** — strong reputation, often faster and cheaper than PSA, with detailed
  subgrades available. Its Pokémon market share has grown a lot, though PSA 10s still
  tend to sell for more.
- **BGS (Beckett)** — famous for strict grading and its coveted **Black Label** (a
  perfect 10 across all four subgrades). Popular for modern high-end cards; turnaround
  and cost can be higher.

## Grading scales

All three use a 1–10 scale, but the top end differs:

- **PSA** — whole grades up to 10 (Gem Mint). No public subgrades.
- **CGC** — up to 10, with a rare **Pristine 10** and **Perfect 10**; subgrades available.
- **BGS** — half-point grades (e.g. 9.5 Gem Mint) with four subgrades (centering,
  corners, edges, surface); the 10 Black Label is the pinnacle.

## What actually matters for resale

For Pokémon, **PSA 10 tends to have the deepest buyer pool and the strongest premium**,
which is why it dominates the high end. CGC can be the smarter value on turnaround and
fees, especially for mid-tier cards. BGS shines for modern cards where a Black Label or
9.5 carries prestige. The right choice depends on the card's value tier and how quickly
you want it back.

## When grading is (and isn't) worth it

Grading only pays when the PSA 10 (or equivalent) premium clears the fees and shipping —
and when the card is genuinely high grade. For low-value cards, grading usually costs
more than it adds.
