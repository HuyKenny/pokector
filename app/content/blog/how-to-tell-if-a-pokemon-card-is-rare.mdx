The quickest way to tell how rare a Pokémon card is takes about two seconds: look at
the tiny symbol in the bottom corner. But the symbol is only half the story — set,
print run and condition matter just as much. Here's how to read a card properly.

## The rarity symbol (bottom of the card)

On most English cards, the rarity symbol sits in the bottom-left or bottom-right
corner, next to the card number. The classic three:

| Symbol | Rarity | Roughly means |
|---|---|---|
| ● Circle | Common | Most cards in a set |
| ◆ Diamond | Uncommon | Less frequent |
| ★ Star | Rare | One or two per pack |

A black star means a standard rare; a card with a **holofoil** finish and a star is a
holo rare. From there, modern sets stack on higher tiers — often shown as multiple
stars, a double-diamond, or lettered symbols depending on the era:

- **Double Rare** (two stars) — modern V / ex cards.
- **Ultra Rare** — full-art and V/VMAX/ex cards.
- **Illustration Rare** and **Special Illustration Rare** — the sought-after "alt art"
  cards with extended artwork.
- **Hyper Rare / Gold Rare** — gold, metallic cards, usually the highest number in a set.
- **Secret Rare** — a card numbered *above* the printed set total (e.g. 201/195).

## Details that make a card actually valuable

The symbol tells you the rarity tier; these tell you the money:

- **Set and era** — a Base Set holo is worth far more than a modern holo of the same
  rarity. Check the set symbol and the card number (like `4/102`) to identify the set.
- **First Edition / Shadowless** — early Base Set cards with a "1st Edition" stamp, or
  the shadowless printing, carry big premiums.
- **Secret rares and chase cards** — the alt-art and gold cards drive most of a modern
  set's value.
- **Condition and grading** — a PSA 10 can be worth many times a raw copy of the same card.
