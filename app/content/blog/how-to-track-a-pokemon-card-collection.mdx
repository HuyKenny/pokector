Most collections start the same way: a shoebox, then two, then a binder, then the
question — _what do I actually have?_ Here is a system that works whether you own
fifty cards or five thousand.

## Decide what "tracked" means for you

Before entering anything, pick your level of detail:

- **Inventory level** — card, set, and quantity. Fast, good enough for set completion.
- **Collector level** — adds printing variant (normal, holofoil, reverse holofoil, 1st
  edition) and condition. Needed for any meaningful valuation.
- **Investor level** — adds what you paid and when. This unlocks profit/loss tracking,
  and your future self will thank you for it.

You can mix levels: track bulk commons at inventory level and your binder cards at
collector level.

## Record the variant, always

Two copies of the same card number can differ in value by an order of magnitude
depending on the printing. When you add a card, note whether it is the normal print,
holofoil, reverse holofoil, or a 1st edition printing. Market prices are published
per variant — a tracker can only value what you tell it you own.

## Be honest about condition

Grade your own cards on the standard scale: Near Mint, Lightly Played, Moderately
Played, Heavily Played, Damaged. Check edges, corners, surface, and centering under
good light. Condition doesn't change the _market estimate_ a tracker shows (that
number describes typical near-mint raw sales), but it changes what your copy would
really sell for — record it so you know which numbers to trust.

## Enter purchase prices while you still remember them

The single most common regret among collectors who start tracking late: lost cost
data. If you can reconstruct what you paid — order histories, receipts, memory —
enter it per card, including the date. Partial data is fine; a good tracker computes
profit/loss only over cards where both a cost and a current price exist, so you never
distort your numbers with guesses.

## Make maintenance a habit, not a project

- Add new cards the day they arrive; a two-minute habit beats a weekend backlog.
- After trades or sales, update quantities immediately.
- Once a quarter, spot-check a binder page against your records.
