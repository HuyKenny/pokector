Every collector eventually asks why the "price" of the same card looks different on
every site. The short answer: there is no single price — only measurements of a
living market. Here is how to read them.

## Where the numbers come from

Most English-card pricing traces back to large marketplaces, primarily **TCGplayer**
in the United States (USD) and **Cardmarket** in Europe (EUR). They publish several
statistics per card and printing variant:

- **Market price** — a weighted view of what copies _actually sold for_ recently.
  This is the most useful single number.
- **Low / mid / high** — the range of current _listings_. Low tells you the cheapest
  ask, not what cards sell for; treat it as a floor, not a value.
- **Trend / average sell** (Cardmarket) — the European equivalents, in euros.

Pokector, like most trackers, prefers market price and falls back to mid, then low,
when market data is missing — and shows you which source and date each number comes
from.

## USD and EUR are different markets

The US and European markets move independently. A card can be plentiful in Europe
and scarce in the US. That is why serious tools never blend USD and EUR into one
number — Pokector shows Cardmarket EUR separately, unconverted, for reference.

## The variant is half the price

"Charizard from Base Set" is not a price — _which printing?_ Normal, holofoil,
reverse holofoil, 1st edition? Each variant has its own market. When a source
publishes no price for a variant, an honest tracker labels it **unpriced** instead of
guessing from a different variant.

## Raw prices assume near-mint

Published market prices describe **raw (ungraded)** cards in typical near-mint
market condition. A moderately played copy usually sells at a significant discount;
graded cards trade in an entirely separate market with their own prices. Keep this
in mind whenever you read a portfolio total.

## Reading price history honestly

Two rules protect you from wishful thinking:

1. **Only real observations count.** A chart should begin when tracking began — a
   line drawn through dates when nobody measured anything is fiction.
2. **Short windows mislead.** A 7-day spike on a thinly traded card can be one
   ambitious listing. Trust 30-day and longer trends more, and volume-heavy cards
   most.

Prices are information, not instructions. Collect what you love; let the numbers
keep score, not set the goal.
