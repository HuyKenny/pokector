"How much is my Pokémon card worth?" has one honest answer: whatever someone will pay
for that exact card, in that exact condition, right now. Here's how to find that number
instead of guessing.

## Step 1 — Identify the exact card

Two cards with the same Pokémon can be worth wildly different amounts. Pin down:

- **The set** — read the small set symbol near the bottom, or the set name.
- **The card number** — the `number/total` (e.g. `4/102`) identifies the print.
- **The rarity** — the corner symbol (● common, ◆ uncommon, ★ rare and up). Not sure?
  See our guide on [how to tell if a card is rare](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-pokemon-card-is-rare).
- **Special traits** — 1st Edition stamp, shadowless, promo, or reverse holo.

## Step 2 — Judge the condition honestly

Condition is often the biggest factor in value. Look at corners, edges, surface
scratches and centering under good light. A near-mint card can be worth several times a
played one — and be honest, because buyers will be.

## Step 3 — Understand raw vs graded

- **Raw (ungraded)** prices are what most cards trade at day to day.
- **Graded** cards (PSA, CGC, BGS) in high grades can command large premiums. A PSA 10
  of a valuable card can be worth many times the raw copy — which is exactly why people
  grade. Run the numbers first with our
  [grading calculator](/pokemon-card-grading-calculator).

## Step 4 — Check the live market (not a fixed "book value")

Card prices move constantly, so a single number from a years-old guide is useless. Use
current market data: the TCGplayer market price (US) and Cardmarket trend (EU) reflect
recent sales.

The easiest way: [search your card on Pokector](/cards). Each card page shows the live
market price for every printing and variant, plus a price-history chart so you can see
whether it's rising or falling. To gauge whether your card is a big one at all, compare
it against the [most valuable Pokémon cards](/most-valuable-pokemon-cards) and watch the
[biggest price movers](/price-movers).
