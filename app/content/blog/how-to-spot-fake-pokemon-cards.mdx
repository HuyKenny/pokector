The higher a card's value, the more counterfeits chase it. The good news: most fakes
fail several simple checks at once. Here's the checklist collectors actually use.

## The quick physical checks

- **Texture and finish** — genuine holos have a distinct, even foil pattern. Fakes
  often look too glossy, too flat, or have holo where the real card doesn't.
- **Print quality** — under magnification, real cards are crisp. Fakes show fuzzy
  edges, dot patterns, or slightly off colors.
- **Font and spacing** — compare the name, HP and attack text to a known-real card.
  Wrong font weight or spacing is a giveaway.
- **The back** — check the blue Poké Ball back against a real card: color shade,
  centering and the swirl pattern are hard to fake exactly.
- **Color of the borders** — the yellow border on many fakes is too dark, too light,
  or uneven.

## The light and rip tests

- **Light test** — real modern cards have a thin black layer in the middle of the
  cardstock. Hold the card to a bright light: a genuine card blocks most of it; many
  fakes let light through.
- **Weight and feel** — fakes are often too thin and flimsy, or oddly stiff.

(Only ever rip-test a cheap card you're willing to destroy — never a card you might sell.)

## The detail checks that catch "good" fakes

- **Set symbol and rarity** — a fake often pairs the wrong set symbol, energy symbol,
  or rarity with the card. Cross-check the real card's set and number.
- **Card number** — verify the `number/total` matches a real print (e.g. `4/102`).
- **Spelling and grammar** — subtle typos in attack text are common on counterfeits.
- **"1st Edition" stamps** — faked constantly on Base Set cards; treat any high-value
  first edition with extra suspicion.

The fastest way to confirm a card is real is to compare it to the genuine article.
[Search the card on Pokector](/cards) to see its correct set, number, rarity and
artwork, then match every detail against the card in your hand.

## Where fakes show up most

Counterfeiters target the expensive end of the hobby — the same cards on our
[most valuable Pokémon cards](/most-valuable-pokemon-cards) list. If a deal on a
Charizard or an alt-art looks too good, it usually is. Buy from reputable sellers,
prefer graded copies for big-ticket cards, and check completed sales prices so you know
the real market rate before you pay.

## The bottom line

No single test is perfect, but fakes rarely survive the whole checklist. Run the
physical, light and detail checks together — and when a card matters,
[look it up first](/cards) and consider a graded copy from a trusted grader.
