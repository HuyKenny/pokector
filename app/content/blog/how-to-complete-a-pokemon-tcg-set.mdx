Set completion is collecting with a finish line — and one of the most satisfying
projects in the hobby. It is also where undisciplined budgets quietly disappear.
Here is a plan.

## Define "complete" before you start

- **Base set completion** — one copy of every numbered card (say, 1–102). The
  standard goal.
- **Printed vs. total** — modern sets contain secret rares numbered _above_ the
  printed set size (e.g. 198/197). Decide whether your goal includes them; the
  difference can triple the cost.
- **Master set** — every card in every variant (normal + reverse holo + special
  prints). A serious commitment; know the multiplier you're signing up for.

Write your definition down. Scope creep is the number-one killer of set projects.

## Packs are entertainment, singles are strategy

Opening packs is fun and legitimately worth paying for — as fun. As a _completion
strategy_, expected value is brutal: the last 20 cards you need will almost never
come out of the next booster. The efficient pattern used by most completionists:

1. Rip whatever packs you enjoy early, when every pull is new.
2. Somewhere around 60–70% completion, switch to buying **singles**.
3. Save bulk trades and show visits for the mid-rarity gaps.

## Budget the chase cards first

Most sets follow the same cost curve: commons and uncommons are near-free, rares are
cheap, and 3–10 chase cards are half the total cost of the set. Before committing,
look up the estimated cost of the full missing list — a good tracker sums current
market prices of your missing cards automatically. If the chase cards are out of
reach, consider completing "base set minus secrets" first; it is a real milestone.

## Track relentlessly

A completion project lives and dies by its checklist:

- Mark cards owned the day they arrive.
- Watch the **estimated missing-card cost** trend — buying dips on the expensive
  cards is where real money is saved.
- Put the missing chase cards on a **wishlist with price alerts**, so the market
  comes to you instead of you refreshing listings.

In Pokector, every set page doubles as your personal checklist: owned, missing,
completion percentage, and what the rest would roughly cost today.

## Enjoy the last card properly

Whatever it is — buy the best copy you can afford, sleeve it, place it in the binder
slot that has waited all this time, and take the photo. You earned it.
