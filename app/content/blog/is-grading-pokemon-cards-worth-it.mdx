Grading turns a raw card into a sealed, authenticated, numbered slab — and sometimes
multiplies its value. But grading costs money and takes time, and not every card is
worth it. Here's how to decide, with actual math instead of vibes.

## What grading actually does

A grading company (PSA is the most recognized for Pokémon) inspects a card's corners,
edges, surface, and centering, then assigns a grade from 1 to 10 and seals it. The
grade does two things: it **authenticates** the card and it **standardizes condition**,
so buyers pay a premium for certainty — especially at the top of the scale.

## The PSA 9 vs PSA 10 gap is the whole game

Here's the part beginners underestimate: the jump from PSA 9 (Mint) to PSA 10 (Gem
Mint) is often a **multiple**, not a few percent. A card might be worth $40 raw, $70
in a PSA 9, and $200 in a PSA 10. That gap is the opportunity — and the risk, because
most cards don't earn a 10. A tiny centering flaw or an edge nick you can barely see
caps you at a 9.

## The costs you must subtract

Grading isn't free, and the fees decide whether it's worth it:

- **PSA fee per card** — varies by service tier and the card's declared value.
- **Shipping both ways** — plus insurance on valuable cards.
- **Your time** — turnaround can be weeks or months.

On a $40 card, fees can easily eat the entire upside of a PSA 9. On a $400 card,
they're a rounding error. That's why grading almost always makes sense on expensive,
near-mint cards and almost never on cheap ones.

## Be honest about the grade you'll get

Before you spend a cent, inspect your card critically under good light:

- **Centering** — the most common 10-killer; check front and back.
- **Corners** — any whitening or fraying drops the grade.
- **Edges** — nicks and chipping, especially on holos.
- **Surface** — scratches, print lines, and indentations.

If it's not a clean, well-centered, near-flawless copy, price your outcome as a PSA 9,
not a 10 — and let the calculator tell you if it still pays.

## When grading is worth it — and when it isn't

**Worth it:** expensive cards, near-mint condition, strong PSA 10 demand, cards you'd
sell. **Usually not:** cheap cards, visible flaws, cards you're keeping anyway, or
anything where the fees swallow the premium.
